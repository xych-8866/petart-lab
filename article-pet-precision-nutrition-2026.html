2. Host-Specific Probiotics: The New Frontier

Probiotics have been used in pet food for years, but the 2026 revolution is about host specificity. Research published in Frontiers in Veterinary Science highlights a critical challenge: probiotics that work in one species may not work in another[reference:8]. The biological functions of probiotics exhibit significant strain-specificity and host-specificity, influenced by both the genetic background of the strain and the physiological environment of the host[reference:9].

Current probiotics approved for pet foods include genera like Lactobacillus, Bifidobacterium, Bacillus, Enterococcus, and Saccharomyces — but future precision probiotics will be tailored to individual pets[reference:10].

What this means for your pet: The next generation of probiotic supplements and functional foods will be designed specifically for dogs or cats (or even specific breeds), not just generic "pet probiotics."
