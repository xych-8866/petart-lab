2. What to Look For

  • Independent crash testing (Center for Pet Safety certification) – the gold standard
  • Heavy-duty metal clips – not plastic
  • No elastic bungee sections – allow too much forward movement
  • Harness design that avoids the neck – neck-only attachments cause spinal injuries
  • Easy to install and remove – so you'll actually use it every time

4. How to Choose the Right Restraint

  • Small dogs (< 25 lbs): Booster seat or carrier
  • Medium dogs (25-60 lbs): Crash-tested harness
  • Large dogs (> 60 lbs): Crash-tested harness or cargo barrier
  • Cats: Crash-tested carrier (soft or hard-sided)
  • All pets: Choose CPS-certified products for maximum safety

5. How to Acclimate Your Pet to Car Restraints

  1. Introduce the harness/carrier at home with treats
  2. Let them wear it for short periods before driving
  3. Take short drives and reward calm behavior
  4. Gradually increase drive length
  5. Never punish fear; use positive reinforcement

7. What to Avoid

  • Attaching to a collar instead of a harness – can break the neck in an accident
  • Elastic tethers – allow too much forward movement
  • Non-crash-tested products – provide little to no protection
  • Using a seatbelt clip without a harness – can cause injuries

8. Your Action Plan
