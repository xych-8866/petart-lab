📊 Prevalence: Allergies affect up to 20-30% of dogs and 10-15% of cats. Environmental allergies (atopy) are the most common type, but food allergies account for approximately 10-15% of cases.

2. Breakthrough Treatments in 2026

Injectable Monoclonal Antibodies

Befrena (tirnovetmab): A new anti-IL-31 monoclonal antibody injection approved for the treatment of canine allergic dermatitis and atopic dermatitis[reference:1]. Clinical studies report initial effectiveness within 24 hours of injection and itch relief that lasts six to eight weeks[reference:2].

Oral JAK Inhibitors

NUMELVI (atinvicitinib tablets): The first and only second-generation Janus Kinase (JAK) inhibitor for the control of pruritus associated with allergic dermatitis in dogs[reference:3]. As a once-daily oral tablet, it offers convenient administration for dogs aged six months and older, supporting both acute and chronic dermatitis management[reference:4].

Zenrelia (ilunocitinib): Another JAK inhibitor option for managing allergic pruritus in dogs[reference:5].

Other Medications

  • Cytopoint (lokivetmab): Injectable biological therapy that targets the protein that sends itch signals to the brain, given every 4 to 8 weeks as needed[reference:6]
  • Apoquel (oclacitinib): Oral JAK inhibitor for managing allergic itch[reference:7]
  • Cyclosporine: Immunosuppressant for severe cases[reference:8]

3. Food Allergy Diagnosis: The Elimination Diet

As of 2026, the elimination diet remains the only validated diagnostic tool for confirming food allergy, as no serological or salivary test can confirm it[reference:9]. An elimination diet involves:

  • Feeding a novel protein diet (e.g., duck, venison, rabbit) or hydrolyzed protein diet for 8-12 weeks[reference:10]
  • If symptoms resolve, reintroduce the original diet to confirm the allergy
  • Strict avoidance of the offending ingredient(s) long-term

4. Environmental Management

  • Reduce allergen exposure: Wash bedding weekly, use HEPA air purifiers, wipe paws after walks[reference:11]
  • Bathe your pet regularly: Bathing with a hypoallergenic shampoo can reduce allergens on their skin and fur[reference:12]
  • Use allergy relief products: Medicated shampoos, sprays, and foams[reference:13]
  • Consider immunotherapy (allergy shots): The only treatment that can modify the underlying allergy[reference:14]

5. When to See a Veterinary Dermatologist

  • If symptoms don't improve with over-the-counter remedies
  • If you suspect food allergies and want to pursue an elimination diet
  • If your pet has recurrent ear infections or skin infections
  • If your pet is severely itchy, impacting their quality of life
  • To discuss new treatment options like Befrena or NUMELVI

7. What to Avoid

  • Self-diagnosing: Food allergies can only be confirmed with an elimination diet[reference:15]
  • Over-the-counter human antihistamines: Without veterinary guidance
  • Stopping treatment prematurely: Allergies require ongoing management
  • Ignoring underlying causes: Allergies can be managed but not always cured[reference:16]

8. Your Action Plan

  1. Differentiate environmental vs. food allergies (seasonality, signs, age of onset)
  2. If you suspect food allergies, start an 8-week elimination diet with a novel or hydrolyzed protein
  3. If environmental allergies are suspected, reduce allergen exposure and consider new 2026 treatment options
  4. Work with your vet or a veterinary dermatologist for a comprehensive treatment plan
