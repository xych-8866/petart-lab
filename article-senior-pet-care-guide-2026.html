3. Nutrition for Aging Pets

According to Interzoo 2026 and SuperZoo 2026, "functional nutrition" and "longevity" are reshaping pet food[reference:3][reference:4]. Key nutritional strategies for senior pets:

  • Joint support: Glucosamine, chondroitin, MSM, and omega-3 fatty acids
  • Kidney support: Lower phosphorus, high-quality protein, added B vitamins
  • Cognitive support: MCT oils, antioxidants, DHA/EPA
  • Digestive health: Prebiotics, probiotics, easily digestible proteins
  • Weight management: Lower calories, higher fiber to maintain healthy weight

5. End-of-Life Care and Making the Decision

As pets age, end-of-life care becomes an important consideration. Key aspects include:

  • Hospice and palliative care: Focus on comfort and quality of life when cure is no longer possible
  • Pain management: NSAIDs, opioids, gabapentin for chronic pain
  • Nutritional support: High-calorie, easily digestible foods for pets with reduced appetite
  • Quality of life assessment: Use the HHHHHMM scale (Hurt, Hunger, Hydration, Hygiene, Happiness, Mobility, More good days than bad)
  • Euthanasia: A peaceful, painless end when quality of life has declined

Signs it may be time: Chronic unmanageable pain, inability to eat/drink, loss of interest in beloved activities, difficulty breathing, more bad days than good.

7. What to Avoid

  • Ignoring subtle changes: Small changes in behavior, appetite, or activity can signal serious issues
  • Waiting for annual check-ups: Senior pets need semi-annual veterinary visits
  • Overlooking dental health: Dental disease affects overall health in senior pets
  • Delaying end-of-life decisions: Quality of life should be the priority
