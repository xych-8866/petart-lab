When 6-year-old Chihuahua, Bella, started dropping food and pawing at her mouth, her owner found a loose tooth. The vet diagnosed advanced periodontal disease and recommended extraction of 4 teeth. Bella underwent surgery and recovered well. "I was nervous about the surgery," her owner says, "but she's so much happier now without the pain."

📊 Prevalence: Tooth extractions are one of the most common surgical procedures in small animal veterinary practice. By age 3, over 80% of dogs and 70% of cats have some form of dental disease that may require extraction.

1. Why Are Tooth Extractions Necessary?

  • Advanced periodontal disease: Bone loss around the tooth roots makes teeth loose and painful
  • Fractured teeth: Cracked or broken teeth with exposed pulp cause pain and infection
  • Retained baby teeth: Deciduous teeth that don't fall out can cause overcrowding
  • Tooth resorption: Common in cats – the body breaks down the tooth structure
  • Malocclusion: Misaligned teeth causing trauma to other teeth or soft tissues
  • Oral masses: Tumors or cysts that require tooth removal

2. Simple vs. Surgical Extraction

TypeDescriptionComplexity
Simple ExtractionTooth is gently lifted and removed using elevators and forcepsLow (single-rooted, loose teeth)
Surgical ExtractionRequires flap elevation and bone removal. Often for multi-rooted teethHigh (multiple roots, ankylosed teeth)

3. The Procedure: What to Expect

  • Pre-anesthetic bloodwork: Ensures your pet is healthy enough for anesthesia
  • Dental X-rays: Essential to assess root health and bone loss
  • Anesthesia: General anesthesia is required for all dental extractions
  • Procedure: Extraction, cleaning, and suturing (if needed)
  • Recovery: Your pet will wake up with pain medication and possibly antibiotics

4. Post-Operative Care

  • Soft food: Feed soft food for 7-14 days after extraction
  • Pain medication: Administer as prescribed
  • Antibiotics: If prescribed, complete the full course
  • No chewing toys: Avoid hard toys and bones during recovery
  • Monitor for complications: Swelling, bleeding, or signs of pain
  • Follow-up: Recheck appointment in 10-14 days

5. Costs

The cost of tooth extraction varies widely depending on the complexity and number of teeth. Typical ranges:

  • Simple extraction: $500-$1,500 (includes cleaning and X-rays)
  • Surgical extraction: $1,000-$3,000 per tooth
  • Full mouth extractions (cats): $1,500-$4,000

Pet insurance often covers dental extractions if they are medically necessary.

6. What to Avoid

  • Delaying extraction: Can lead to more severe infection and bone loss
  • Hard foods during recovery: Can damage the extraction site
  • Skipping post-op check: Delayed healing can go unnoticed
  • Ignoring signs of infection: Swelling, discharge, or pain

7. Your Action Plan

  1. If your pet shows signs of dental pain, schedule a veterinary exam
  2. Discuss X-rays and treatment options with your vet
  3. Prepare for post-operative care (soft food, medications)
  4. Monitor recovery and attend follow-up appointments

Final thought: Tooth extractions are a common, safe procedure that dramatically improves your pet's quality of life. The cost of extraction ($500-$3,000) is a worthwhile investment in your pet's health and comfort.
