📊 Prevalence: Ringworm (dermatophytosis) is one of the most common fungal skin infections in dogs and cats. It's highly contagious and can spread to other animals and humans (zoonotic). Young, elderly, and immunocompromised pets are at higher risk.

1. What Is Ringworm?

Ringworm is a fungal infection caused by dermatophytes – fungi that feed on keratin (the protein in hair, skin, and nails). Despite the name, ringworm is not caused by a worm. The infection causes circular, scaly, hairless lesions that may or may not be itchy.

Common fungal species: Microsporum canis (most common in cats), Microsporum gypseum, Trichophyton mentagrophytes.

3. Signs of Fungal Skin Infections

  • Circular bald patches – characteristic of ringworm
  • Scaly or crusty skin – in or around the affected area
  • Redness and inflammation
  • Itching (may be mild or absent)
  • Broken hairs – hairs that break off at the skin surface
  • Lesions on ears, face, paws, and tail
⚠️ Ringworm is contagious to humans. If your pet has ringworm, wash your hands after handling them and keep them isolated from other pets.

4. Diagnosis

  • Wood's lamp examination – some fungal species fluoresce under UV light
  • Fungal culture – collecting hair samples for laboratory testing (gold standard)
  • Skin cytology – to rule out bacterial infections
  • PCR testing – rapid DNA-based testing for dermatophytes

5. Treatment Options

Topical Therapy

  • Lime sulfur dips – effective but may be messy
  • Topical antifungal creams and sprays – miconazole, clotrimazole, terbinafine
  • Medicated shampoos – chlorhexidine, miconazole, ketoconazole

Oral Therapy

  • Itraconazole – most common oral antifungal for cats and dogs
  • Terbinafine – effective alternative
  • Fluconazole – used in some cases

Environmental Decontamination

  • Vacuuming regularly – fungal spores can survive in the environment
  • Disinfecting surfaces – bleach solution (1:10) can kill spores
  • Washing bedding – in hot water with antifungal detergent

7. What to Avoid

  • Using human antifungal creams on pets – may contain harmful ingredients
  • Stopping treatment early – fungal infections require full treatment courses
  • Ignoring environmental cleaning – spores can cause reinfection
  • Not isolating infected pets – can spread to other animals and humans

8. Your Action Plan

  1. If you notice circular bald patches, see your vet promptly
  2. Follow treatment protocols (topical + oral if needed)
  3. Clean your home thoroughly (vacuum, disinfect, wash bedding)
  4. Isolate infected pets to prevent spread
  5. Continue treatment until follow-up culture is negative

Final thought: Fungal infections like ringworm are common, treatable, and preventable. The cost of treatment ($50-$200) is much less than the cost of chronic skin issues and the risk of spreading to family members. Early diagnosis and consistent treatment are the keys to a full recovery.
