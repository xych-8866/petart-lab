1. The Challenge: Too Many Images, Too Few Radiologists

Diagnostic imaging — including MRI, CT, X-ray, and ultrasound — is essential for veterinary clinical evaluation[reference:4]. But the growing demand for veterinary diagnostic examinations has created a gap between the volume of images produced and the capacity to evaluate them[reference:5]. AI is stepping in to bridge that gap[reference:6].

2. AI in Veterinary Diagnostic Imaging: The Current Landscape

Recent literature shows extensive use of AI models in veterinary imaging, particularly in dogs[reference:7]. These models have proven especially effective in:

  • Auto-segmentation of magnetic resonance images — automatically identifying and outlining structures in MRI scans[reference:8]
  • Classification of thoracic lesions — identifying abnormalities in chest X-rays[reference:9]
  • Detection of cardiac pathologies — using radiography to identify heart conditions[reference:10]
  • CT and MRI evaluation of otitis media — providing the most complete diagnostic picture in ear infections[reference:11]

3. Breakthrough: AI-Powered Heart Murmur Detection

In April 2026, Boehringer Ingelheim and Eko Health launched an innovative AI-based solution to detect, visualize, and grade heart murmurs in dogs[reference:12]. The CANINEBEAT® AI algorithm detects heart murmurs associated with structural heart disease in dogs with more than 95% sensitivity and specificity[reference:13].

How it works: The Eko CORE™ Digital Attachment connects to most single-tube stethoscopes, digitizes heart sounds, and amplifies them by 40 times[reference:14]. The AI algorithm, trained on more than 4,000 annotated canine heart sound recordings, provides clinical support and helps veterinarians discuss findings with pet owners[reference:15].

With heart disease affecting approximately 10% of all dogs, and MMVD being the most common heart disease in dogs, this tool represents a major advancement in early detection[reference:16].

4. AI-Powered Dental Radiograph Analysis

Antech Diagnostics launched RapidRead Dental for Feline in early 2026 — an AI-powered dental radiograph interpretation tool designed specifically for cats[reference:17]. The software analyzes dental radiographs and generates tooth-by-tooth annotations, helping veterinarians identify pathologies such as periodontitis and tooth resorption[reference:18].

  • Training data: More than 55,000 images and 275,000 teeth[reference:19]
  • Speed: Dental evaluations in less than 10 minutes[reference:20]
  • Feature: Automatic charting using the Triadan tooth numbering system[reference:21]

This is particularly valuable because feline dental radiographs are challenging due to the small size of feline teeth and the high prevalence of tooth resorption[reference:22].

5. Emerging AI Diagnostic Tools

  • AI pet health monitoring from photos: Samsung, in collaboration with Lifet, is developing an AI-powered pet care solution that analyzes photos for signs of cataracts, dental diseases, and patellar luxation — with up to 97% accuracy claims[reference:23][reference:24]
  • Nanoparticle-enabled diagnostics: Ultrasmall iron-oxide nanoparticles for lymphotropic MRI achieved very high specificity (94-96%) in dogs with head/neck tumors[reference:25]
  • At-home viral detection: Researchers have developed test strips for quick detection of FPV (feline parvovirus) and CPV (canine parvovirus), similar to at-home COVID-19 or flu tests[reference:26]
  • Mast cell tumor testing: IDEXX is rolling out comprehensive mast cell tumor testing for dogs, with FNA cytology on the IDEXX inVue Dx™ Cellular Analyzer[reference:27]
